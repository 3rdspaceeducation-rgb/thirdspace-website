Transnational Education

Australian qualifications,
delivered globally

Transnational Education (TNE) is the delivery of Australian higher education programs outside Australia. Students enrol at a partner institution in their home country and study toward a fully accredited Australian qualification — the same degree, the same standards, the same outcome — without leaving home.

For Australian institutions, TNE is a proven pathway to international growth, brand recognition, and sustainable offshore revenue — without the capital commitment of a branch campus.

Delivery Models

Structured pathways
for every context

01
Third-Party Delivery

The Australian registered provider retains full responsibility for curriculum, academic standards, and the awarding of qualifications. The offshore institution delivers the program to students on the ground in their home country.

  • Australian provider expands reach without a campus overseas
  • Offshore partner gains access to a prestigious Australian curriculum
  • Third Space manages compliance coordination and operational alignment
  • Cost-effective, low-risk market entry model
02
Twinning Programs

A student completes part of their Australian degree at a local institution in their home country, then transfers to the Australian campus to finish the remainder — most commonly a 2+1 or 2+2 arrangement.

  • Students earn a fully recognised Australian degree
  • Cost savings of up to 60–70% vs studying the full degree in Australia
  • Gradual transition — students build confidence locally first
  • Third Space coordinates the partnership and pathway support
TNE Delivery Options

Every model,
explained simply

TNE is not one-size-fits-all. The right model depends on your institution's goals, risk appetite, and the regulatory environment of your target market.

01
Articulation Agreements

One of the most common and lower-risk models. A partner institution overseas delivers a diploma or partial degree, then students transfer into the Australian institution with credit recognition.

Lower regulatory complexityStrong recruitment pipeline
02
Credit Transfer Partnerships

A formal agreement recognising units completed at one institution as equivalent to units at another, allowing students to gain credit toward an Australian qualification for prior local study.

Accessible entry pathwayReduces time-to-degree
03
Dual Award / Double Degree

Students enrol at both an Australian institution and an offshore partner, completing a co-designed program that results in two separate awards — one from each institution simultaneously.

High prestigeDual recognition
04
Franchise / Licensed Delivery

The Australian institution licenses its curriculum, brand, and academic framework to an offshore provider who delivers the full program locally. The Australian institution awards the qualification.

Scalable revenueNo campus investment
05
Offshore / Branch Campus

The Australian institution establishes a physical presence overseas, delivering its own programs on the ground. Highest brand presence and control — significant capital and regulatory commitment.

Full brand controlMaximum market presence
06
Teaching / Study Centres

A lighter-footprint alternative to a branch campus. A local centre hosts face-to-face tutorials, exams, and student support while the program remains managed from Australia.

Low capital outlayPhysical student touchpoint
07
Online TNE / Hybrid Delivery

Programs delivered fully or partially online, enabling Australian institutions to reach students anywhere without physical infrastructure. Hybrid models combine online with local face-to-face components.

Unlimited geographic reachHighly scalable
08
Microcredentials & Executive Education

Short, focused credentials targeting professionals who need specific skills without committing to a full degree. Increasingly in demand across Asia as industries modernise rapidly.

Fast time-to-marketStackable toward full awards

Regulatory Framework

TEQSA & Compliance

Australia's Tertiary Education Quality and Standards Agency (TEQSA) regulates higher education providers and ensures TNE arrangements meet the same rigorous quality standards as domestic delivery.

TEQSA

New TNE Authorisation Requirements — Effective 1 January 2026

The Education Legislation Amendment (Integrity and Other Measures) Bill 2025 has introduced significant changes to how TNE is regulated in Australia.

Authorisation Required

From 1 January 2026, all registered higher education providers must obtain TEQSA authorisation before delivering Australian courses at offshore premises.

Standards Framework

All TNE must comply with the Higher Education Standards Framework (Threshold Standards) 2021, with a self-assurance report demonstrating compliance across all offshore delivery.

Ongoing Obligations

Providers must maintain notification and annual reporting obligations. Non-authorised delivery of Australian awards offshore may attract civil and criminal penalties.

ⓘ
Third Space is compliance-ready. We support partner institutions through the full TEQSA compliance lifecycle — from initial authorisation applications to ongoing annual reporting.
1
Governance & Accountability

Awarding institutions retain full accountability for curriculum, assessment, and academic outcomes regardless of offshore delivery model.

2
Quality Assurance

All offshore programs must meet the same quality thresholds as domestic delivery, with robust monitoring in place.

3
Student Protections

Institutions must demonstrate equivalent support and academic resources for offshore students as for onshore students.

4
Self-Assurance Reporting

TEQSA authorisation requires a comprehensive self-assurance report demonstrating readiness across all Threshold Standards.
